# Retro-futurism: `/design style retro-futurism`

I choose one past vision of tomorrow and use it consistently. The style needs an era, not a random pile of science-fiction effects.

---

## Required companion reference

I follow the [style workflow](style.md) for report context, scope, implementation and verification. The command loads it alongside this guide.

---

## The design pass

- I state the direction in one sentence: for example, a 1960s space-age travel desk or a 1980s orbital computer. I pick one before choosing tokens.
- I pick one era of vision. Googie and Atomic Age (1950s–60s) bring upswept roofs, boomerangs, starbursts and chrome. Cassette futurism (1970s–80s) brings analog screens, beige hardware and amber phosphor.
- I derive the palette and shapes from that direction. Space-age can use cream, orange and capsule forms; an electronic direction can use dark ink, phosphor accents and instrument rules.
- I use display lettering for short titles and a readable companion for body text. Labels describe real actions rather than invented cockpit jargon.
- I limit chrome, glow, orbital diagrams or scanlines to supporting decoration. Text and controls stay crisp with solid backing.
- I keep native navigation and familiar forms. Decorative radar or gauges must not imply live data unless they display real values.
- I disable flicker and remove nonessential movement for reduced motion. I verify contrast, focus and mobile reading order with every decorative layer enabled.
